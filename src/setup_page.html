Who the phone calls

Each button on the phone calls one person, and the light next to it is on when they can be called. You can fill in one to four.

Call length

When a call reaches this length the phone hangs up and says "Time's up". The child can press the button again to call back.

Emergency calls

Leave it empty to turn this off (the default). When it is on, pressing the same button this many times in a row, each press less than 1.5 seconds after the last, calls that person even at bedtime or outside their available times. Just before the call the phone plays a short rising tune, so you can tell your child: "If you really need me, keep pressing my button until you hear the music." 15 to 20 is a good number: high enough that playing with the buttons does not set it off. A person who is available is called on the first press as usual. While this is on, more quick presses do not hang up a call the button just made; to hang up, wait a moment, then press. It still needs WiFi and a phone that is set up.

Voice language

Every sentence the phone says ("They didn't answer the phone", "Time's up", bedtime, secrets) uses this language. The sentence Twilio plays when nobody answers follows it too (after a phone software update, press Run setup again once). Play a sample says one sentence on the handset.

Bedtime

During bedtime every light is off, even for people who are available, and pressing a button says "It's sleep time. You can call in the morning." instead of calling. A call that is already going is not cut off. Your Call buttons on this page still work.

Secret messages

A button can record a secret: your child talks into the handset and the phone emails the recording to that button's person, as a sound file, from your own email account. Nothing goes through Twilio or anyone else. Pick which buttons do this under Who the phone calls.

Email account

How to get a Gmail app password

An app password is a separate 16 letter password Google makes for one device, so the phone never needs your real Gmail password. It takes about two minutes, best done on a computer.

  1. Sign in to the Gmail account the secrets should come from, then open myaccount.google.com/security.
  2. Under "How you sign in to Google", turn on 2-Step Verification if it is off, and follow Google's steps (usually a text code to your phone). Google only offers app passwords with it on.
  3. Open myaccount.google.com/apppasswords (type it in the address bar; it is hard to find in the menus). Google may ask for your password again.
  4. Under "App name" type Kid Phone and press Create.
  5. Google shows a box with 16 letters in four groups, like abcd efgh ijkl mnop. Copy it into App password below and press Save. The spaces don't matter.
  6. Press Done. Google never shows it again; if you lose it, delete it on the same page and make a new one.

If the apppasswords page says the setting is not available for your account: 2-Step Verification is still off, the account is a work or school account whose admin blocks app passwords, or it signs in only with a passkey or security key (add a phone number as a second step). Changing your Google password deletes every app password, so make a new one after that.

The phone only uses the app password to send mail. It can't change your account settings, and deleting it on the apppasswords page switches secrets off. Some providers (possibly Outlook.com and Hotmail) may not allow app passwords; any provider with SMTP login works.

Limits

A press of the same button ends a secret early. Past the hourly limit the button plays the busy tone.

Buttons and lights

Which pin on the phone's board each button and its light are wired to. Use the GPIO numbers printed next to the pins. A button goes from its pin to GND. A light goes from its pin through a 330 ohm resistor to the LED's long leg, and the short leg to GND.

Every pin is in use. To add another button, set one of the lights to No light to free its pin.

A light is optional. A button with no light still calls, it just can't show when the person is available or that it's bedtime. Changes work as soon as you press Save, no restart needed.

Connect to Twilio

The phone uses your Twilio account to make calls. You need an account with one phone number (Twilio charges about $1 a month for the number plus a few cents per minute).

  1. Open console.twilio.com in another tab and log in.
  2. On the home page, find Account Info.
  3. Copy the Account SID and the Auth Token (click Show first) into the boxes below.

Used once, then forgotten: the phone never stores it. Instead the phone keeps a limited Twilio key that can only update the list of numbers it may call, so changing names, times or phone numbers later does not need the token.

      Advanced

      Factory reset

      Erases everything on the phone: the WiFi, the Twilio connection, the people on each button, their times, the call length, bedtime and the button pins (back to buttons on GPIO 4-7 and lights on 15-18). Nothing in your Twilio account is deleted.

      Afterwards the phone is off every saved WiFi network, so this page stops working. Set it up again from the start: plug its COM port into a computer and open the installer page.

      You can also erase all settings by holding the BOOT button on the phone's board for 10 seconds.
